# Fiber Art Suppliers
Not a comprehensive list!

I'm US-based, so for availability reasons in a cursed polity, I'm noting *location*; some non-US-based suppliers (e.g.) may not ship physical items to the USA at any given point in time.

- [Ashford Wheels and Looms](https://www.ashford.co.nz) - New Zealand-based. Ashford's wheels are ubiquitous in the USA and carried by many retailers, as well as being relatively common on the secondhand market (although this is regional). I own and have used a secondhand Ashford Traveller and Ashford Traditional. They're great wheels; I think of them as the Honda Civics of the spinning wheel world, not "flashy" but absolutely reliable. Even for older examples (folks on Ravelry dated my Traditional as being from 1981!), you can readily obtain replacement parts, which is great for those of us buying secondhand.
- [AVL Looms](https://avllooms.com) - US-based. I don't expect these ever to be within my reach, but I like poring over the pages!
- [Blazing Shuttles](https://www.blazingshuttles.com/) - US-based. Sumptuous hand-dyed warps and skeins, plus other tools.
- [Claudia Wollny](https://www.claudia-wollny-edition.com/en/) - Germany-based. Excellent books on tablet weaving and kumihimo, available in Deutsch/English (sometimes more, depending on the specific book!) bilingual editions.
- [Collins Custom Pro](https://www.etsy.com/shop/CollinsCustomProLLC) - US-based, Etsy. As far as I know, they only sell one thing but it's *so useful*: an adjustable loom warp tensioner you use with clamps for warping by yourself. (You have to supply the dowels, but you can get dowels of a correct length for your loom.) I've used this with the Toika Laila but I believe it would work fine with e.g. a table loom. There are similar tools available from other sellers, like [Purrington Looms'](https://www.purringtonlooms.com) [Angel Wings](https://www.purringtonlooms.com/Angel_Wings.htm); this is one I landed on, alongside a similar set from [Handywoman](https://www.handywomanshop.com).
- [Creo Press](https://creopress.eu) - EU-based. Carries [Peggy Osterkamp](https://peggyosterkamp.com)'s weaving books, available in PDF or hardcopy.
- [Daedalus Spinning Wheels](https://www.daedalusspinningwheels.com/) - US-based. I'm not in the market for another e-spinner (yet?), but I have a [Penelope band loom](https://www.daedalusspinningwheels.com/penelope), which can be set up for either card/tablet weaving or rigid heddle weaving. The Penelope is very well designed although I'm just barely starting a project on it. They have at least one e-spinner design for left-handers as well as right-handers, and sometimes they carry silk for spinning; I haven't yet spun any from the order but it looks very promising!
- [Dharma Trading Co.](https://www.dharmatrading.com/) - US-based supplier, especially but not exclusively for silk and dyes (particularly fiber reactive dyes), silk painting supplies etc.
- [Dreaming Robots](https://www.dreamingrobots.com/) - US-based. E-spinners and other comparatively affordable tools. (Alas, "comparatively affordable" is still pricey in this space.) I have the [EEW 6.1](https://www.dreamingrobots.com/eew-61/) e-spinner and like it a lot. It's friendlier if you have mobility issues around the feet/legs or need to fit your spinning equipment into a much smaller space than a typical treadle wheel.
- [Emerald Erin](https://shop.emeralderin.com) - Canada-based. Patterns, kits, and supplies for underwear and swimear making.
- [Eugene Textile Center](https://www.eugenetextilecenter.com/) - US-based. Their used books and fiber tools on the online store have delicious finds; they also offer yarns, spinning wheels, looms, *new* books and magazines, more.
- [Fancy Kitty](https://www.fancy-kitty.com) - US-based. Great fiber processing (and other) tools. I have the smaller bench-top wool picker and it's sturdy and very well designed, including a safety lock; if I didn't have a Strauch drum carder already (secondhand) I would be looking at them for one of those!
- [Feral Scene](https://feralscene.com/) - US-based supplier, semi-local to me. Their art batts for spinning are great. Also carries [Spinolution](https://www.spinolution.com/) spinning wheels and accessories. (I have a Mach III.)
- [Fiber Artist Supply Co.](https://www.fiberartistsupply.com/) - US-based. More affordable tools beautifully and thoughtfully made. I have the yarn swift, bobbin winder, and cone holder.
- [Fossil Fibers](https://www.fossilfibers.com) - US-based. Some delightful colorways. I discovered with regret that I cannot for the life of me comfortably spin Icelandic, but they do have other wool/blend varieties!
- [Gargoylelover (Etsy)](https://www.etsy.com/shop/gargoylelover) - US-based. Beautiful art batts, also semi-local to me (they're in Texas).
- [Gist Yarn](https://www.gistyarn.com) - US-based. Lovely weaving yarns and patterns/kits; I've used the Beam cotton in 3/2 and am looking forward to trying others.
- [Golding Fiber Tools](https://dropspindle.info) - US-based. I love looking but I will likely never be able to afford any of these beautiful heirloom wheels.
- [Handywoman](https://www.handywomanshop.com/) - US-based. Gorgeous handmade weaving tools, including looms, shuttles, and more. The shuttles are to die for, and she has some terrific ultra-slim ones as well as specialty shuttles for band looms etc. If you're an embroiderer or sewist, check out the wooden embroidery cards!
- [Inagaki Kiryo Weaving Supplies](https://inagakikiryo-weaving.com) - Japan-based. They have recently updated their website and *web store* to add an in-depth English-language version of the site! I have an [R-25 floor loom](https://inagakikiryo-weaving.com/collections/handloom/products/handloom-r25) (counterbalance, 6H 6T) that was built to order (approximately three months). Excellent communication; there's at least one person on staff who speaks English although I'd initially sent in my order in both Japanese (carefully copy-pasted and checked via Google Translate) as well as English, before the English-language website storefront was added. Assembly was quite straightforward; [details](https://github.com/maratai/craftery/blob/main/fiber_arts/loom_%20inagakikryo_R-25.md). I'm currently warping for a First Weaving on this loom! As with any floor loom, this is on the spendy side, including international freight *and customs/duties*. They also carry both bamboo and metal reeds at very fine dents (for metal, up to ~180/10 in cm!). I covet the [warping drum (48 cm)](https://inagakikiryo-weaving.com/collections/warping-board-%E3%82%B3%E3%83%94%E3%83%BC/products/warping-drum-48cm) someday if I am very good, but a number of other weaving/spinning tools look intriguing.
- [IxChel](https://ixchel.com.au/) - Australia-based. Spinning fibers (*amazing* fiber preparation - the wallaby batts are almost better than CENSORED to spin), plus yarns and tools.
- [Jane Stafford School of Weaving](https://www.schoolofweaving.tv) and [Jane Stafford Textiles](https://janestaffordtextiles.com) - Canada-based. Noting for future exploration. The School of Weaving has video courses; Jane Stafford Textiles carries supplies from looms to kits and yarn.
- [Jenkins Spindles](https://jenkinsspindles.com) - US-based. Exquisite Turkish spindles, although they sell out almost instantly so you have to be vigilant if you want one! A more budget-friendly/easier-to-source option is [Snyder Spindles](https://www.etsy.com/shop/SnyderSpindles), also available from multiple retailers.
- [JMS Wheels](https://www.jmswoodwork.com/product.html#/) - US-based, with the octagonal Cassandra Spinning Wheel. Long lead time (12+ weeks) as they're made to order at a more affordalbe price point. I am on a spinning wheel moratorium or I'd be tempted; I'm on the waitlist for a Pocket Wheel (mine is on long-term loan to a friend), and I have a Spinolution Mach III, Louët S90, Ashford Traveller, itoguruma, and EEW 6.1 sitting in the house. I love the design, though!
- [John Galen Designs](https://www.johngalen.com) - US-based. Beautiful handcrafted spindles and spinning tools; I stan for his amazing tahkli-style supported spindles, which are available in plainer/more budget-friendly variations as well as glorious, semi-customizable fancier designs.
- [Lendrum Spinning Wheels](https://lendrumwheels.ca) - Canada-based, back from the ashes. I have not had the opportunity to try a Lendrum wheel although it's on my list!
- [Level Wind Spinners](https://myautowinder.com/) - US-based. Makes the terrific LWS autowinder for the Pocket Wheel, among others. Powered by a couple of AA or AAA batteries, but doess not require the purchase of special bobbins or add friction to the spin.
- [Louët](https://louet-inc.odoo.com) - Site is for the North American distributor; the company is Netherlands-based. I was fortunate enough to purchase an out-of-production Louët S90 secondhand and have her set up with a [WooLee Winder](https://www.wooleewinderstore.com). (WooLee thoughtfully contacted me to double-check since the S90 was last produced in *2015* and he wanted to be certain I was purchasing the correct model!) She has my favorite treadling experience of the wheels I've used, although she has a hilariously aggro take-up and would have made me cry when I was starting out. (My first wheel was a patient, steady Ashford Traveller, an excellent teacher).
- [Manufaktur Weiden](https://manufaktur-weiden.de/michi/) - Germany-based, I believe, with the Reisespinnrad Michi. I don't believe they ship internationally but the design is extremely neat to look at.
- [The Miller Girls Yarn](https://themillergirlsyarn.com/) - US-based. Terrific wholesaler with broad and unusual offerings.
- [Pocket Wheel](https://www.pocket-wheel.com/) - US-based small workshop maker of Pocket Wheel travel spinning wheels. I have a secondhand one and it's amazing. The wait list is two years long but the wheel is *fantastic*, especially if you want an ultraportable wheel. Check the ergonomics; I'm 5'4" and suspect this would be less comfortable for people with longer legs or greater height due to the sitting position required to operate the wheel.
- [Real Fibers (Suzy Ballenger)](https://realfibers.com/rf/store/). US-based, I think; will double-check later. Noting to investigate.
- [Saori Santa Cruz (Etsy)](https://www.etsy.com/shop/saorisantacruz) - US-based (although Saori looms are imported from Japan). No affiliation, but I am a very happy repeat customer. Jill has her own tutorials for Saori looms and tools that I found invaluable.
- [Saori Studio Los Angeles](https://saoristudiola.com/) - US-based (again, Saori looms are imported from Japan). No affiliation, a very happy repeat customer. Laura is terrific to work with.
- [School of SweetGeorgia](https://www.sweetgeorgia.co/) - Canada-based. Video lessons in fiber arts (rigid heddle and multi-shaft weaving, spinning, dyeing, also knitting and crochet); they also sell supplies.
- [Séguin Looms](https://seguinlooms.com/?lang=en) - Canada-based. Site is in English and français. I have not had much chance to explore Fiberworks yet. I'd love to hear from someone who has used a Séguin computerized dobby loom as they appear to be scarce in the USA.
- [Selvedge Magazine](https://www.selvedge.org/) - UK-based, I *think*. I was fortunate enough to purchase a number of back issues from someone who was destashing, which have made for tremendous reading; I hope someday I can justify splurging on a subscription!
- [Snyder Spindles](https://www.etsy.com/shop/SnyderSpindles) - US-based. Terrifically reliable Turkish spindles. (I haven't tried the others. I *really* like Turkish spindles!)
- [Spinnrad - HENKYS](https://spinnrad-henkys.de) - Germany-based spinning wheels, I believe. I'm unlikely ever to get to see one in person but I like to dream!
- [Spinolution](https://www.spinolution.com/) - US-based. Fantastic spinning wheels although the "toe treadling" requires some practice. Also accessories; the niddy noddy is notably great. I have a secondhand Mach III with an 8-oz flyer and the 4A accelerated flyer, and it's probably my Forever Wheel as the 4A makes spinning silk easier. (Ratios up to 73:1 on the Monarch although my Mach III may "only" get up to 52:1.) The toe-treadling action on the Mach III is ls pleasantly light, but may not suit everyone.
- [SpinPerfect](https://spinperfect.com/) - US-based. I haven't tried the 3D-printed spinning wheel, but they make accessories and bobbins for Spinolution spinning wheels. I have a Mach III with the SpinPerfect PaulyWinder installed on the Spinolution flyers and it's great. The PaulyWinder will work with the bobbins you already have, but these flyers are *only* made for the SpinPerfect wheels.
- [Texsolv](https://texsolv.se/) of Texsolv heddle fame (weaving). Sweden-based.
- [TheDancingGoats](https://www.etsy.com/shop/TheDancingGoats) - Etsy, US-based. A terrific variety of tools; I've used the rigid heddles.
- [Toika](https://shop.toika.com/) - Finland-based. Looms and other tools. From contacting them, I learned that in the USA they distribute via [WEBS Yarn](https://www.yarn.com/) so contact WEBS to place an order. I have a Toika Laila (8H 8T) and adore her; I did have to ask Toika directly for an updated [Toika Laila assembly manual PDF](https://github.com/maratai/craftery/blob/main/fiber_arts/LAILA_LOOM.pdf), which I've uploaded here, but if it doesn't match *your* Laila, best to email Toika asking for the correct one.
- [Treenway Silks](https://www.treenwaysilks.com/) - US-based supplier, fantastic mulberry and tussah silk for spinning. The sample cards are absolutely worthwhile. Also other varieties and blends I haven't tried yet.
- [Vävstuga Weaving School](https://vavstuga.com) - US-based. They specialize in Swedish weaving looms and equipment, with a terrific selection of books.
- [Weberei Hamburg](https://www.weberei-hamburg.com/en) - Germany-based? I've picked up the English-language translations of some of the loom construction and plan books, available as PDFs; I'm not a carpenter but they're delightful to pore over.
- [WEBS Yarn](https://www.yarn.com/) - US-based supplier. I have a [Toika Laila loom](https://www.yarn.com/pages/toika-looms) (8-harness 8-shaft) from them. [Toika Laila listing on Toika's website](https://shop.toika.com/category/6/laila-loom); Toika itself is Finland-based but WEBS is their official distributor in the USA.
- [Woodington UK](https://woodingtonuk.com/) - UK-based maker of looms and weaving tools. I have the [folding tablet loom](https://woodingtonuk.com/products/folding-tablet-loom) and it's gorgeous; if I weren't in the US, I would be ordering more from here.
- [WooLee Winder Co.](https://www.wooleewinderstore.com/) - US-based. Flyers that wind on evenly for you, mechanical. I have one set up on my Louët S90 and enjoy it a lot. These do require that you buy the matching geared bobbins for use with the WooLee Winder, and add a bit of extra take-up.
- [The Woolery](https://woolery.com/) - US-based retailer. A staggering variety of offerings.
- [The Woolgatherers](https://www.woolgatherers.com/ONLINECATALOG.htm) - US-based. Terrifically informative, especially regarding looms.
- [Yarn Barn of Kansas](https://www.yarnbarn-ks.com) - US-based. Terrific source of weaving, spinning, and other supplies, including books. Great selecton of weaving yarns. Carries the *Weaver's Craft* magazine, too.

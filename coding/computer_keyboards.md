# Keyboards I Have Used, not a complete list
My bias is toward mechanical keyboards and I type *hard*; I learned to touch type (100 wpm) on a manual typewriter and old habits die hard. (Yes, I have typewriters, but that's a separate post.)

- **iClever**. Often finicky but they have a range of folding/ultraportable keyboards.
- **Epomaker Luma40**. This is my first Epomaker, and it's a 40% hyper-compact ortholinear model. I'm looking forward to finding out if I can adapt to it or not! The keys do feel tremendous so I may also commit a larger-sized Epomaker.
- **Keychron** is much beloved by many but I got two units with stuck key issues and gave up.
- **Kinesis** has a bunch of ergonomic keyboards. The contoured ones (e.g. Advantage) are a learning curve but they're terrific for RSI issues. The split keyboard (Freestyle) is probably an easier entry point. They're spendy, though.
- **Logitech**. So much Logitech. A lot of affordable + reliable keyboards. I like the **Logitech Casa**, which is a "work from home" travel keyboard + trackpad combo that one can put away into a clever box that doubles as a stand.
- **ProtoArc Foldable Keyboard and Mouse, XKM01 True Full-Size Folding Wireless Keyboard Mouse Combo for Business Travel** (charges over USB-C, separate keyboard and mouse connecting over USB-A wireless dongle or Bluetooth, has a very sensibly designed travel case and tablet/phone stand). I picked this up to be a travel "full-sized" keyboard for the rpi. Terrific design, A+++++. The keyboard is folding and has shallow key travel BUT it is just large enough to include a numpad. Honestly I love this if I don't need the additional laptop stand of the Logitech Casa (which is a great all-in-one travel kit because I have three very dead brain cells so tracking SEPARATE mouse or trackpad plus keyboard plus stand usually results in me FORGETTING A THING).
- **Qwerkywriter**. Spendy but beautiful. I finally indulged in a keyboard *and* the number pad (the "black gold" colorway; I was too cheap to indulge in the beautiful "white gold" one), and I love mine. The main keyboard has a stand that'll hold e.g. a tablet securely. You can switch between USB-C or Bluetooth. Also, the carrying case is the most wonderfully overengineered, well, carrying case I've ever seen. No regrets.
- **Royal Kludge** I've had one defective unit (sadly, it was the folding travel one, although that was heavier than I could cope with anyway) but otherwise, these are terrific mechanical keyboards and the RK89 is a daily driver for me. I've gotten the mechanical keyboards, which also support Bluetooth and have fun LED light nonsense. :3 I love these, although these tend to be solid, heavier devices - not quite as much "built like tank" factor as the Kinesis but I don't tend to travel with them. These ship from overseas to the USA and tend to be a bit slow to fulfill even in the Before Times or I would own more of them.
- **Samsers Foldable Bluetooth Keyboard with Touchpad** (Bluetooth, charges over USB-C). This is comparatively light although not *very* light. Works great; IIRC the touchpad and its mouse-buttons work over Linux. No numpad, very shallow key travel, somewhat smaller even when folded out, but a great travel option.
- **Serounder 2.4GHz Touchpad Keyboard, A8 Mini Rechargeable Ultra-Thin Mini USB** (wireless via 2.4GHz USB-A dongle, charges via USB-C, very smol). This is really more of a keypad and I suspect it's intended for gaming devices like the XBox? Regardless, surprisingly nice clicky feel. I haven't yet gotten the touchpad to work with the rpi (I suspect a driver issue) but the wireless dongle just works, which is a relief. This is likely what I use for the rpi build. The dongle also docks in the keyboard itself so one is less likely to lose it. (Other keyboards have this too, I'm just too tired to remember which).
- **Vilros VILP108** keyboard with touchpad (USB-A, wired). This is a very slim, very lightweight wired keyboard + touchpad over USB-A. The keyboard has no numpad and the keys are very shallow but pleasant. The touchpad works fine. When I need a "nearly full size but no numpad" travel keyboard, this one is usually it.

# Me and Keyboards
- I learned on a manual typewriter. I *bang* on the keys. Accordingly, I've accepted that I wear out keyboards a few times a year. This is job-related, so it's part of a cost of doing business.
- I prefer mechanical keyboards and love the satisfying clicky feel.
- I don't mind slim keyboards as long as they don't feel *mushy*.
- I can't tolerate persistent "stuck keys." At that point the keyboard gets binned.
- I'm fine with trackpads when built-in although I usually use mice.
- I *strongly* prefer arrow keys to have an inverted-T or similar layout. I cannot generally cope with the arrow-keys-in-a-horizontal-row nonsense.
- I can tolerate the weird "Fn key + $WHATEVER" nonsense, especially on lighter/smaller/portable keyboards as a space-saving measure, although I don't love it. For a full-sized keyboard, I strongly prefer actual fucking extra keys, including Home and End.
- For most use cases, I don't need a separate numpad. (Sibelius uses them integrally as keyboard shortcuts for NOTE ENTRY.) In fact, I own dedicated numpad devices. So lack of numpad is not a dealbreaker.
- I dislike Bluetooth because I have rotten luck getting devices to pair reliably, but I do have some Bluetooth-only or Bluetooth-added devices for Reasons.
- I mostly have 2.4GHz wireless for the "extra" computing devices (Micro Journal, rpi) because I lose the dongles and USB-A is a Problem on fucking MacBook Pro. :] I do find that, unlike Bluetooth, these tend to just work.
- For "full-sized" or even "mostly full" keyboards, I cannot cope with ortholinear layouts. For ickle thumb-typing keyboards, it's fine.
- I type in QWERTY (US) but did at one point achieve 40 wpm in Dvorak because, uh, boredom and/or grad school procrastination. But in real life, QWERTY.
- I have not yet tried a chording keyboard. I'm intrigued by them, though.

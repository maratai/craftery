# Cryptology
I realize it's modern practice to use "cryptography" as an umbrella term for codes, ciphers, cryptography, cryptanalysis, and steganography techniques altogether, but indulge me here.

- Paul Garrett. *Making, Breaking Codes: An Introduction to Cryptology*. 2001. This is a great introductory text *from the math side*; it assumes that you're familiar with or comfortable with some undergraduate-level number theory and abstract algebra; it's less suitable for general readers without a STEM background. Among other things, quadratic residues may be of mathematical interest but even as a hobbyist I tend to doubt they're widely used as a serious cryptographic technique! This is also bound to be quite dated regarding modern developments; you'd want a more recent text to discuss e.g. post-quantum cryptography. **Recommended**, with the noted caveats.
- Heiko Knospe. *A Course in Cryptography*. 2019. This is an undergraduate-level American Mathematical Society text that approaches cryptology (etc) with less "pure math" rigor and more of a broader practical brush for STEM majors who may not specifically be *math* majors. You'll also find an introductory discussion of quantum computing (e.g. Shor's algorithm). You'd probably want to supplement this if you're after a strictly rigorous mathematical proof-based approach. **Recommended**, with the noted caveats.

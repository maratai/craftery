# Tabletop Roleplaying Games
I've broken TTRPGs into their own catewgory because, well.

- *Dialect: A Game About Language and How It Dies*.
- *The Epic of Dreams: Basilisk Edition*. A rare example of modulo arithmetic in game mechanics. I have not played it but I'm intrigued.
- *Gun&Slinger*. This touts itself as "separat[ing] the Western genre from it's [sic] long history celebrating ideals of genocide and colonialism" (11), in particular calling out the genre's historical dependence on "The Other," while displacing the allegedly deprecated "The Other" to a fictive It's Always Malevolent Evil force ("The Twist") with no apparent sense of irony. Some interesting ideas; but you know, "We decided to go in on Western [genre] roleplaying" is fine without logically inconsistent ethical tap-dancing. (I say this as someone most of whose TTRPG experience is *Pathfinder* of "murder hobo" fame since that's one of the games popular in my area.) I'm more than usually frustrated because (a) I love games that get into the ethics of roleplaying (John Tynes' *Power Kill* is an excellent metagame with commentary on "murder hobo" gamer tropes and was one of my introductions to the topoi) and (b) gunslinger TTRPGs are one of my interests, but (c) here the execution re: (a) leaves something to be desired.
- *The Lost and the Jammed: High lethality gungeoncrawling*. Interesting revolver-inspired (graphically, anyway) "dice chain" mechanic in a science fantasy postapocalyptic gunslinger setting.
- *Power Kill/Puppetland*. A twofer by John Tynes, 1996. *Power Kill* is notably a metagame commenting scathingly on the "murder hobo" strand of gaming arising from e.g. Dungeons & Dragons. Tynes has generously made [*Power Kill* available online](https://johntynes.com/revland2000/rl_powerkill.html); *Puppetland* has a revised edition.
